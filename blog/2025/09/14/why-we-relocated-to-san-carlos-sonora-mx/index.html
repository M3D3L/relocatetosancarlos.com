Why Our Family Moved to San Carlos, Sonora

Why our family moved from Ciudad Obregón to San Carlos: our first home, remote work, school routines, and the everyday details that made it feel like home.

Why we chose San Carlos

After years of working remotely, we wanted more than a change of scenery. We wanted a place to put down roots, buy our first home, and raise our two daughters. San Carlos, Sonora, offered the coastal setting we loved and a daily routine we could picture making our own.

We had spent six months in Vancouver, Canada, before living in Ciudad Obregón. We enjoyed those experiences, but we were ready to settle somewhere that felt right for our family.

Buying our first home

During our search, the homes we considered in San Carlos made living near the beach feel within reach. Our purchase took six months from start to finish. While we waited for the school year to end before moving, we rented out the house on Airbnb. We were first-time homeowners and first-time hosts, learning both as we went.

We chose a home in Ranchitos San Carlos, near the highway to Guaymas. That location helped with the school commute. A nearby gym, grocery delivery to our address, and the community around us also made the house a practical choice, not just a place with a view.

If you are starting your own search, our homes for sale in San Carlos offer a starting point. Compare each location with the errands and commutes you will make regularly, and confirm which services reach the address.

A routine built around work and family

A typical school run takes us about 40 minutes. Once I am home, I work remotely for around six hours, sometimes picking up smaller tasks in the evening. My husband and I both work in tech, so a home that supported our work mattered as much as the coastal lifestyle. Our guide to internet options in San Carlos covers questions worth asking before you move.

We cook most meals at home and prepare food ahead of time. In the afternoons, our daughters attend art classes in town and take Japanese lessons online. On weekends, we try to make room for a hike, a beach visit, or another local outing.

The small moments that make it home

Taking the girls to watch the sunset has become one of our favorite rituals. Living near the Sea of Cortez makes those outings part of everyday life rather than something we save for a vacation. We still choose our beach visits around local weather and water conditions.

Our surroundings were another reason for moving. In Ciudad Obregón, my husband and youngest child struggled with allergies and sinusitis. We lived near a tire recycling center, and our doctor recommended a more humid environment with less smog. That advice shaped our family's decision; it is not medical advice for anyone considering a similar move.

As someone born and raised in Sonora, I also wanted our daughters to stay connected to their roots while meeting people from different backgrounds. San Carlos gives us opportunities to share our traditions and learn from others.

How to turn a scouting visit into a useful trial run

A holiday shows you one side of San Carlos; a relocation visit needs to show you an ordinary weekday too. Alongside time at the beach, leave room to drive likely school routes, shop for your usual groceries, and see how a possible neighborhood fits your working hours. Those details are easier to judge in person than from listing photographs.

For a family working remotely, a beautiful room is only part of the picture. Think about where two people could take calls, how school pickups would fit around meetings, and whether the house has a comfortable place to work. Ask the owner about the actual internet installation and utility arrangements instead of assuming every home offers the same setup.

  • Try your daily routes: travel between the homes you are considering, possible schools, and the shops you would use.
  • Ask address-specific questions: confirm delivery coverage, internet availability, and any maintenance or community fees.
  • Compare housing with your whole budget: a location that changes your driving or school choices may also change your monthly spending.
  • Leave room to adjust: if you are undecided about an area, consider whether an initial rental would help you make a more informed purchase.

None of this takes away from the appeal of living by the sea. It gives that appeal a practical foundation. For us, the move was about building a life that could hold work, school, family time, and the small outings we enjoy together.
