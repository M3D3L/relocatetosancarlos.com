How to Make Friends in San Carlos, Sonora, MX

New to San Carlos, Mexico? Discover easy ways to meet people and make friends, from joining local gyms and clubs to exploring coffee shops and community groups.

How to Make Friends in San Carlos as a Newcomer

Moving to a new town can be exciting, but let’s be honest—it can also feel a little lonely at first. When we first arrived in San Carlos, I didn’t know anyone outside my family. But little by little, through everyday activities and casual interactions, I started meeting wonderful people who made me feel at home.

The good news is that San Carlos has one of the friendliest communities you can imagine. Locals and expats are welcoming, and because it’s a small town, it’s easy to start recognizing familiar faces in no time. Here are a few easy ways to make friends and connect with the community without too much effort.

Join the Local Gym

There is only one gym in town, and that makes it one of the best places to meet people. It’s called Athletic’s Club, and it offers a variety of activities, from yoga and zumba to water aerobics, spinning, and strength training.

What I love most about this gym is that people don’t just go to work out—they go to socialize. The classes are very community-oriented, and you’ll find both Spanish-speaking and English-speaking groups who are always happy to welcome newcomers.

Even if you’re not big into fitness, the group classes are worth trying just for the friendly atmosphere. It’s one of those easy, low-pressure ways to meet people organically while doing something good for yourself.

Try Pickleball or Padel

Pickleball and padel are huge in San Carlos right now. These sports have become very popular among both locals and expats, and the best part is that they’re naturally social.

Clubs often post their class schedules and tournament announcements on social media, so you can easily stay up to date. I usually repost local events and tournament updates on my own pages, so feel free to follow my social accounts if you want to stay in the loop.

Even if you’re a beginner, don’t be sh. Most players are welcoming and happy to teach new people. You’ll find yourself part of the local sports community before you know it.

Connect Through Church

If faith is part of your life, you’ll find several active church communities here in San Carlos. Most are Catholic, but there are also Christian and Mormon congregations, among others.

Church groups tend to organize family-oriented gatherings and events, which can be a wonderful way to connect with others who share your values. Even if you’re not a regular churchgoer, attending a community event can be a great opportunity to meet people and get involved.

Visit Coffee Shops and Local Restaurants

Some of the best conversations I’ve had in San Carlos started over brunch or coffee.

Cafes and restaurants are great spots to meet people, especially during brunch hours when the town feels most alive. At some point, you’ll probably find yourself exchanging smiles or small talk with the table next to you. If people start seeing you around regularly, they’ll usually strike up a conversation and welcome you to the area.

Coffee shops tend to attract digital nomads and remote workers, so if you work online, you might meet others doing the same (or racing for the wall sockets). Most cafes and restaurants offer free WiFi and a relaxed environment that makes it easy to connect naturally.

Get Out and Explore Town

Sometimes, the easiest way to meet people is simply by being out and about.

Take walks around the Marina at Marina Terra, visit El Mirador for the views, or stroll along the beachfront by the main boulevard. These are popular spots where locals walk their pets, enjoy the views, or just hang out.

You’ll notice that during the cooler months, especially in fall and winter, more people are outside enjoying the fresh air. A friendly smile or a simple “hello” often leads to casual conversations and even friendships.

Join Online Groups and Forums

San Carlos has an active online community that makes it easy to stay informed and meet people before even leaving the house.

Start by joining Facebook groups like “What’s Up San Carlos.” You’ll find event announcements, local recommendations, and sometimes even people organizing get-togethers.

There’s also a fun Spanish-English conversational group that meets at Colibrí’s Café. It’s a relaxed way to practice languages and make new friends at the same time.

Just browsing online or commenting on posts often leads to local connections—you’ll be surprised how fast you can become part of the conversation.

Final Thoughts

San Carlos might be small, but it’s full of opportunities to meet great people. Whether you prefer group activities, social sports, faith-based communities, or casual conversations over coffee, you’ll find your circle here.

What helped me the most was simply showing up—saying hello at the gym, joining a local class, or attending a small event. Before long, you start to feel like part of the community.
