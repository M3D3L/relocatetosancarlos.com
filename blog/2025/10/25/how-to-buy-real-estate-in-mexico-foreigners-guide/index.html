How to Buy Real Estate in Mexico as a Foreigner

From a local agent in San Carlos, Sonora

Buying Real Estate in Mexico as a Foreigner: A Simple Guide for Buyers in San Carlos, Sonora

Buying property in Mexico can feel confusing at first, especially if you’ve heard about restricted zones and trust structures. The good news is that the process is straightforward once you understand how it works, and thousands of foreigners purchase property safely every year. If you are looking at San Carlos, you’re in one of the most attractive and secure areas for foreign buyers. Sonora also offers the Only Sonora program, which allows free automotive transit for up to six consecutive months, making it even easier for U.S. and Canadian buyers to travel by car while owning a home here.

Why Foreigners Can Buy Property in Mexico

Foreigners are allowed to own property in Mexico, including beachfront property, through a structure called a fideicomiso, or trust. This applies to homes within the Restricted Zone (50 km from the coast and 100 km from international borders), which includes San Carlos. This system has been in place for decades and is widely used by international buyers.

What is a Fideicomiso?

A fideicomiso is a property trust created through a Mexican bank. Even though the bank technically holds the title, you are the sole beneficiary with full control. You can sell, remodel, rent out, pass the property to your heirs, or use it however you like. It functions very similarly to ownership. 
A fideicomiso is valid for 50 years and can be renewed indefinitely. The bank simply acts as a neutral trustee and has no say in how you use your property.

How the Buying Process Works

Connect with a Local Expert
Your first step is choosing a real estate agent who understands foreign transactions, fideicomisos, and local regulations. This guidance ensures your purchase is safe and compliant.

Discuss Payment Type
Foreign buyers have two ways to pay for a property: upfront payment or financing through a mortgage.

a) Paying upfront: You cannot pay the full amount in cash due to Mexico’s “Ley Antilavado,” which helps prevent money laundering. A bank deposit must be made when you sign the trust and deed at the notary. Some cash can be used, but only within legal limits. Paying upfront may also give you room to negotiate a better price and a faster closing. 

b) Financing through a mortgage: To qualify, you must be a temporary resident or hold another valid immigration status. You must provide proof of income from the U.S. along with a credit score report. Additional paperwork may be required, such as a criminal background check or a co-signer. Mortgages typically require 20% down, with the remainder financed over 15 to 20 years. Banorte is one of the banks that offers mortgage options for foreigners.

Make an Offer
Once you find the right property, your agent drafts a written offer that includes price, timelines, contingencies, and items included in the sale.

Open Escrow and Conduct Due Diligence
After the offer is accepted, funds are placed into a secure escrow account. Your agent and a notary begin verifying property documents, ownership records, and closing requirements.

Set Up the Trust (Fideicomiso)
Your agent coordinates with a bank to establish your property trust. The bank becomes the legal titleholder, while you remain the sole beneficiary with full rights over the property.

Sign and Close at the Notary
On closing day, you review and sign your fideicomiso agreement and deed with the notary. Once the documents are registered, the property is officially yours.

Costs Involved in the Process

While fees vary by region and property type, here are common closing costs:
• Appraisal (Avaluo)
• Trust Setup Fee (one-time)
• Annual Trust Fee
• Notary Fees
• Acquisition Tax (2–4% depending on municipality)
• Escrow Fees (promesa de compra-venta)
• Legal review or attorney fees (optional)
Your agent can estimate total costs before you make an offer.

Why San Carlos is a Great Choice for Foreign Buyers

San Carlos is known for its safety, U.S. and Canadian-friendly community, and beachside lifestyle. Sonora’s Only Sonora program also makes it easy for foreign homeowners to drive their vehicles into the state for up to six months without additional permits.

Sonora borders Arizona, and San Carlos is only about a 6-8 hour drive from cities like Tucson or Phoenix. The area offers clear processes, experienced professionals, and a stable environment for real estate investment.
