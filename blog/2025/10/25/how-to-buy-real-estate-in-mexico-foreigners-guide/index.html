Buying Property in San Carlos, Mexico as a Foreigner

A practical introduction to buying in San Carlos as a foreigner: fideicomisos, document checks, payment arrangements, closing costs, and professional advice.

Start with the property and your circumstances

If you are considering a home in San Carlos, Sonora, understanding how the purchase will be structured matters as much as finding a property you like. Discuss the specific property, intended use, and your circumstances with a Mexican notary and, where appropriate, independent legal counsel.

This guide introduces questions to ask. It is not legal, tax, immigration, or financing advice and does not guarantee that any transaction, property, or investment is safe.

Ask about the restricted zone and fideicomiso

For residential purchases in coastal areas such as San Carlos, foreign buyers commonly discuss a bank trust known as a fideicomiso. The bank acts as trustee, and the buyer's rights and responsibilities are set out in the trust documents.

Have the notary and bank explain the structure required for your transaction, who the beneficiaries will be, the trust's term and renewal process, and what is required to sell, rent, transfer, or inherit the interest. Do not assume a trust removes land-use restrictions, community rules, or other legal obligations.

Questions to resolve before closing

  1. Choose your professional team. Ask an agent about foreign-buyer experience and establish who handles legal review, notarial work, and bank coordination. An agent's involvement does not replace independent advice.
  2. Check the documents. Have qualified professionals review title, ownership records, relevant restrictions, outstanding obligations, and the requirements for completing and registering the transaction.
  3. Agree the offer in writing. Set out price and currency, items included, deadlines, conditions, and what happens if a condition is not met. Understand any deposit before sending funds.
  4. Confirm payment arrangements. Ask the notary about lawful payment methods, documentation, and applicable anti-money-laundering requirements. If escrow is proposed, verify the provider and written terms; an escrow service is not the same document as a purchase agreement.
  5. Review the trust and closing documents. Confirm bank requirements, fees, beneficiary details, and signing procedures. Ask the notary to explain registration and which documents you should receive.

If you need financing

Ask lenders directly whether they currently lend to buyers in your circumstances. Eligibility, residency documents, income evidence, down payment, currency, term, and rates depend on the lender and product. A general online example is not a financing offer or approval.

Request an itemized closing-cost estimate

Ask for a current written estimate in MXN and confirm what applies to your transaction. Depending on the purchase, the discussion may include:

  • Appraisal and document-related costs.
  • Bank trust setup and ongoing fees.
  • Notarial and registration charges.
  • Applicable acquisition taxes.
  • Escrow costs if that service is agreed.
  • Independent legal review.

Ask each professional how fees are calculated, when payment is due, and what is included. This article does not quote a fixed tax percentage, closing budget, or purchase timetable.

Plan your visit separately from your purchase

Owning a home does not by itself settle immigration or vehicle-import requirements. If you plan to drive a foreign-plated vehicle to Sonora, confirm current requirements with the relevant authorities for your route, vehicle, and immigration status. Do not rely on a blanket six-month or permit-free rule.
